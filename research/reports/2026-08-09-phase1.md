## What was built

| Determinism harness | SHA-256 manifest compared across two builds | SQL row order, float reassociation, embedded paths (C7) |

### The gate is deliberately harder than the spec's

`CLAUDE.md` §6 asks for one canary that peeks a bar ahead. That canary proves the detector catches
the leak we already thought of. The leaks that cost money are the ones nobody wrote on purpose —
an `open_ts` filter, a z-score fitted on the full period, a session VWAP computed at the close.
The gate here requires one canary per mechanism, and requires the clean features to pass
unflagged, because a detector that fires on everything is no detector at all.

Two probes, and both are needed. Truncation removes post-cutoff rows; perturbation replaces
post-cutoff numbers. Neither dominates: perturbation only touches float columns, so a feature
reading a future *timestamp* walks past it, and a future value entering through a clamp that
usually binds walks past truncation. `tests/test_gate.py` pins one example of each direction so
neither probe can later be dropped as redundant.

## What would falsify the claim being made here

The claim is narrow: *feature code that reads only through `PointInTimeView` cannot see past its
cutoff.* It fails if any of these turn out to be true.

1. A leak mechanism exists that neither probe detects. The most likely candidate is a leak that
   never touches the data root at all — a constant derived from the future and hardcoded, or a
   feature parameter chosen after looking at outcomes. **No detector can catch that**; only
   pre-registration can, which is why Phase 4 exists.
2. Real Rithmic data has a property the synthetic tape lacks that breaks the bar or session logic
   — implied prints, out-of-order timestamps, vendor corrections. Likely. That is what D10 is for.
3. The holiday table is wrong. It is unverified, and a wrong half-day silently truncates a session
   without raising anything.

## Honest limitations

- **Synthetic data has no market structure.** The generator has session boundaries, a volume roll,
  an intraday volatility shape, and jumps. It has no positioning imbalance, no catalysts, and no
  auction. Fitting anything to it would be fitting to a seeded random number generator. The config
  marks `data.version: synthetic-v1`, the pipeline prints a banner, and `build()` raises rather
  than pretending a real snapshot exists.
- **The session calendar is unverified** against CME's published product calendar.
- **Ingest is written against assumed Rithmic properties** (see D10). The parsing layer does not
  exist; the synthetic generator stands in its place.
- **`decision_lag_seconds: 1` is a guess.** If the exchange timestamp turns out to be unavailable
  and only a Rithmic receive time exists, this must grow to cover feed latency.
