01

Is there an edge?

No. None of the 6 context conditions we registered in advance shifts the odds of a big move enough to trade. 0 survived multiple-testing correction, and none reached the 1.5× effect bar set before any of this was measured.

What it shows
The ratio between how often big moves follow the top third of each context measure versus the bottom third. A ratio of 1.0 means the context tells you nothing.
What it means
Every interval straddles 1.0, so none of these readings changes the odds in a way the data can distinguish from chance — and the widest runs from 0× to 9.9× (clipped on the chart, arrow-tipped), which is not a measurement, it is a shrug.
What would change it
More events. These intervals are wide because big moves are rare, not because the method is loose. Roughly four times the sample would be needed to halve them.

02

How big do the moves actually get?

Most go nowhere. The median event travels 0.44× its stop distance before the two-hour clock runs out; one in ten reaches 1.06×. A move worth twice the risk happens 2.1% of the time — 6 times in 292 events.

What it shows
The full shape of what happens after a trigger fires, and how confident we can be about the rare end of it.
What it means
The distribution is heavily concentrated near zero: most triggers are noise. Nothing in this sample reached 6× at all, which is not the same as saying it cannot happen — it means the study has no observations there and cannot distinguish a rare event from an impossible one.
What would change it
A different holding period or stop distance moves the whole picture. Both are set in config and both are reported under a second, pre-declared scaling so a result cannot depend on one lucky choice.

03

Do the setups fire often enough to learn from?

1 of 8 setups fire too rarely to study. Below roughly 150 events a year, a rare outcome cannot be measured at all — there is no statistical repair for a sample that does not exist.

What it shows
How much evidence each setup can ever supply.
What it means
Sample size is the binding constraint on the whole study. A setup at 150 events a year gives about 600 in the training period, and at a 2% tail rate that is a dozen big moves to reason from.
What would change it
Loosening a trigger raises its count and changes what it means. That is a trade, not a free win, and it has to be registered before it is measured.

04

Are there distinct market states?

No. The market states this finds do not survive being re-derived on a different stretch of history, so they describe the period rather than the market. Reported as noise rather than re-tuned until they look stable.

What it shows
Whether grouping events by their market context produces categories that behave differently — and whether those categories are real or accidental.
What it means
A model handed every context variable at once ranks future moves with a correlation of 0.094 out of sample — near zero. R2 of -0.056 is at or below zero, which is the expected outcome and not a failure.
What would change it
Order-flow depth. Every context variable here is derived from trades and the clock; book imbalance and resting liquidity are the obvious missing inputs, and whether they are available is still an open question.

05

Can the machinery be trusted?

The measurement machinery is checked, and it passes. 5 deliberately leaky features were planted and all 5 were caught; all 12 real features came through clean; two independent builds produced byte-identical output.

  • ✓Phase 1Foundation5/5 leak canaries caught, builds byte-identical
  • ✓Phase 2Events & labels292 labelled events, 8 families
  • ✓Phase 3Base ratesdistributions and intervals computed
  • ○Phase 4Hypotheses0 of 6 supported
  • ○Phase 5Structureclusters unstable (ARI 0.16)
  • ◌Phase 6Meta-labelingNo trade log. Phase 6 needs at least 300 logged trades.
What it shows
Whether the pipeline can see the future by accident — the failure that makes a backtest look brilliant and lose money.
What it means
Features read through a handle that physically cannot return data from after the decision moment. The check plants known leaks and confirms they are caught, then confirms the real features are not.
What would change it
A leak that never touches the data — a threshold chosen after seeing outcomes, say. No detector catches that; only registering hypotheses in advance does, which is why the page above leads with them.

Costs assumed before any of this is tradeable: 0.425 points round turn ($21.23 per contract), being commission both sides, a spread assumption, and volatility-scaled slippage on entry and exit. Every interval on this page is a block bootstrap over session-days with 10,000 resamples — events cluster inside a session, and treating them as independent would make every interval here too narrow.
